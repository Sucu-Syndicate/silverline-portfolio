Building a real product is hard in a way nobody warns you about. Not the technical parts — those you can figure out. The hard part is the silence. You're working on something that might actually matter, and most days nobody knows, nobody cares, and there's no signal telling you whether any of it is right. Writing about it changes that. When I write about a decision I made, it stops being a thing I tried and becomes a thing I actually did. It gets real.

Most people wait until they have something to show. They'll write about it when it's done, when it's polished, when it looks like something worth reading. That's the wrong order. The result is the least interesting part. The interesting part is the moment when you're not sure if it's going to work — and you build it anyway. That's what I want a record of.

I'm not writing for an audience. I'm writing so the process has a trail. [YOUR VOICE: rewrite this last line before publishing]
